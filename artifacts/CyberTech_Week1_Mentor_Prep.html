Legacy IoT / OT Security Priority

Old industrial and IoT devices (cameras, PLCs, SCADA, routers) cannot be patched, are often invisible, and are actively used by attackers for botnets, lateral movement and infrastructure disruption. Idea direction: protect the network around the devices (inventory → risk scoring → isolation & monitoring) instead of trying to secure the devices themselves.

Why it hurts

  • 68% of critical infrastructure orgs lack full OT visibility
  • 42% say legacy unpatchable OT is their biggest risk
  • Devices stay 10–20 years; vendors stopped updates long ago
  • Default/hard-coded passwords, unencrypted protocols

Who feels the pain

  • Energy, water, mining, manufacturing, hospitals
  • OT/IT leads and plant engineers
  • Smaller factories & utilities (underserved market)
Recommended focus (from Varduhi’s analysis): Option A — Legacy OT visibility for industrial & critical infrastructure. Clearer pain (downtime + safety) and clearer payer than pure SMB IoT or pure CRA compliance.

Test hypothesis (ready for interviews)

We believe that Armenian infrastructure and industrial organizations do not have an up-to-date list of their OT/IoT devices and firmware versions.
We will interview 5 engineers / IT leads and measure how many cannot state the number of devices and their firmware status.
We are right if ≥ 4 out of 5 cannot.

Deepfake & Money Fraud Strong contender

AI voice & video clones are used for CEO fraud, supplier impersonation and emergency scams. Only ~3 seconds of audio are needed. Losses already exceed $1.5–2.2B globally.

Key numbers

  • FBI: $893M AI-related fraud losses (US, 2025)
  • Average business incident: $145k–$600k
  • High-profile cases: €95M (Fideuram), $25M+ (Arup)

Possible angles

  • Real-time detection inside telecom network
  • Continuous authentication for finance teams
  • Solutions that do not require end-user apps

LLM Application Protection Technical / high upside

Companies deploy chatbots, RAG systems and AI agents faster than they can secure them. Prompt injection remains #1 (OWASP 2026); Excessive Agency rose sharply to #3.

Core risks

  • Prompt injection (direct & indirect)
  • Sensitive data leakage through RAG / tools
  • Agents taking unintended real-world actions
  • Shadow AI and unmonitored agent tool use

Quantum / Post-Quantum Cryptography Supporting angle

Quantum computers will eventually break RSA/ECC. The realistic near-term opportunity is not building quantum hardware or QKD, but helping organizations prepare for the transition.

Realistic for our team

  • High: Cryptographic inventory tools
  • High: Linking quantum risk to legacy IoT devices
  • Medium: Compliance / migration support

Low feasibility

  • Building quantum computers
  • Pure Quantum Key Distribution (QKD)
  • Deep cryptographic research
Recommendation: Do not make quantum a standalone main problem. Use it as extra value for the Legacy IoT direction:
“Our tool shows not only which devices are vulnerable today, but also which ones will not survive the quantum transition.”

Technical Questions for the Mentor

These questions help you understand feasibility, architecture choices and what a realistic MVP looks like for a student team.

  1. Architecture & Approach
    For legacy OT/IoT visibility — is a purely passive (network-listening) approach realistic as an MVP, or do we need some form of agent or active scanning from day one?
  2. Data & Detection
    What are the main practical ways to fingerprint and identify unknown industrial devices without having signatures for every vendor?
  3. Risk Scoring
    How would you recommend prioritising devices? (CVEs + exposure + criticality of the process they control?)
  4. Isolation strategies
    In real industrial environments, how do companies actually isolate risky legacy devices without breaking production? What usually goes wrong?
  5. Team capability
    Given our current mix of skills (one software engineer + people with limited deep IT background), which technical direction is most realistic to prototype in 4–6 weeks?
  6. MVP scope
    What is the smallest useful thing we could show: a discovery + inventory dashboard, a risk report generator, or something else?
  7. Data sources
    Are there open datasets, traffic captures, or lab environments you would recommend for testing OT/IoT discovery ideas?
  8. Deepfake technical path (if the conversation goes there)
    For real-time voice deepfake detection — what are the current practical approaches that can run with low latency (on-device, edge, or network-level)?
  9. LLM security (backup topic)
    For protecting internal RAG / agent applications, where do you see the biggest remaining gaps that smaller teams could still address?
  10. Reality check
    Looking at our shortlist, which problem has the highest chance of a working technical prototype within the incubation timeline, and why?

Suggested 30–40 min Structure

0–5 min

Quick team + context: “We are in Problem Discovery week. We have narrowed to 2–3 directions and need technical feasibility input.”

5–15 min

Present the top 1–2 problems (Legacy OT first, then Deepfake or LLM). Keep each under 4 minutes. Focus on pain + current workarounds + what you are still unsure about technically.

15–35 min

Ask the technical questions above. Let the mentor talk. Take notes on architecture ideas, risks, and what he considers realistic for a student team.

Last 5 min

Clarify next steps: “What should we validate or prototype first this week?” + any introductions he can make.
